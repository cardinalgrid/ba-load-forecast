# Methods

This page states what every piece of the study does, with which parameters, and why that value. The
module docstrings say the same in fewer words; `docs/reproduce.md` lists the commands; the phase
reports (`docs/phase_b.md`, `docs/phase_c.md`, `docs/phase_d.md`) hold the numbers.

## 1. The question and the comparison

Every U.S. balancing authority (BA) reports to the EIA the hourly demand that occurred (`D`) and the
day-ahead forecast it made the day before (`DF`). The study asks whether an open system built from
public data only can match that operational forecast, on ordinary days and at the daily peak during
winter storms. Every model is therefore scored against `DF` for the same BA and day, under the same
information cut-off, and `DF` is scored by the same functions as the models.

The models are developed on ten BAs (PJM, MISO, ERCOT, CAISO, SPP, NYISO, ISO-NE, TVA, Duke Energy
Carolinas, BPA) from 2 December 2020 to 31 December 2022, a period that contains Winter Storms Uri
and Elliott. Winter Storms Gerri and Heather (January 2024) and the January 2025 Arctic events are
not used in any modelling decision.

## 3. Weather stations

One station per BA is the main airport of the largest load centre, the assignment of Cardinal Grid
Note 2. For the development BAs a second table (`stations_multi.py`, `docs/stations_grid_multi.csv`)
adds the airports of the largest cities in each footprint, four or five per BA. Stations are weighted
equally: the additive model and the regression use the mean, the boosting sees the mean and one column
per station. A population weighting was not tested. The HRRR cell is the nearest by great-circle
distance (at most 2.0 km); the ISD record is the one that spans July 2015 to January 2025.

## 4. Evaluation

Per BA and day (`metrics.py`): hourly MAPE over the hours with a positive outcome; error at the peak =
(forecast at the hour of the actual peak − actual peak) / actual peak, taken at the hour of the
**actual** peak so that a forecast peak at another hour is not rewarded; the negative part of that
error (under-forecast); the error in the hour of the peak. A day is skipped with more than two missing
outcome hours or any missing forecast hour.

Aggregation (`report.py`): only BA-days on which **every** model of a report produced a forecast enter
its tables, so that all models are measured on the same days; the number of days, and the value of `DF`,
therefore differ slightly from one report to another (3.00 to 3.03 %). Means are taken per BA and, across
BAs, weighted by each BA's mean demand, as in Note 1. A day is won when the model's MAPE is below the
BA's, or when its under-forecast at the peak is smaller. The Diebold-Mariano test [1] compares daily
MAPE (or peak error) series with absolute loss at horizon one, the small-sample correction of Harvey,
Leybourne and Newbold [2] and a normal p-value (700 to 750 days per BA); a negative statistic favours
the model. Winter events are the calendar days of `grid_sentinel.events`. Special days and their eves
have a table of their own. Quantile forecasts are scored with the pinball loss in percent of the
outcome at the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles, the coverage and relative width of the 5-95 band and
whether the actual peak fell inside it, over all days, the event days and the 30 coldest days of each
BA by observed minimum temperature.

## 4b. Band and tail, apart

The 90 % band answers where the outcome falls on most hours; the tail answers how far it goes when it leaves the band, which the 40 nearest days cannot estimate on their own (two of them lie beyond the 95th percentile). `scripts/tail_analysis.py` (`docs/tail_analysis.md`) therefore judges the two apart on the scored hours of 2023 to 2025: the band by coverage, width and the exceedance rate per side; the tail by the mean excess beyond the bound in percent of the outcome, the worst case, and a generalized Pareto distribution fitted to the excesses of each side with all BAs and years pooled in relative units and then by temperature regime, from which the expected shortfall follows. The tail is also attributed to member disagreement (the top quarter of days by the relative spread of the three members). On 2023 to 2025 with `final4`: coverage 89.2 %, 5.8 % of the hours under the band and 5.0 % over it; mean excess 2.0 % of the outcome below and 1.7 % above, worst 41 % and 33 % (single days at Duke and FPL); Pareto shape 0.19 below and 0.15 above (a heavy but finite-mean tail); in the cold regime the outcome exceeds the upper bound on 7.6 % of the hours (the storm under-forecast) against 5.2 % under it. Days of member disagreement carry 29 % of the hours outside the band and 33 % of the excess, against a quarter by construction: the members signal the tail only weakly.

## 4a. The benchmark by hour of the day

`scripts/df_profile.py` (`docs/df_hourly_bias.md`) profiles the BAs' own forecast (`DF`) against demand (`D`) by local hour, from the EIA-930 files alone. Three things follow for any comparison against `DF`.

1. **At PJM the published `DF` is stamped one hour earlier than `D` from 2019 on.** Compared with the `D` of the following hour, its MAPE falls from 3.4 to 3.9 % to 2.3 to 2.5 % in every year from 2019 to 2025; in 2016 to 2018 the published alignment is the better one (2.3 to 2.4 %). The daily peaks of the two series sit one hour apart. The signed error of `DF` as published has the shape a time shift gives: negative while the load falls (−6 to −7 % from 22:00 to 01:00), positive while it rises (+3 % at 05:00 and 06:00). The same test points to a shift at CAISO in 2016 and from 2023 on, outside or at the edge of the development period, where the CAISO `DF` degrades for other reasons as well (5.5 to 8 %).
2. **Constant or hour-dependent offsets.** NYISO's `DF` is 2.2 to 2.6 % below `D` at every hour and in every year; MISO's is 1 to 3 % above, SPP's 2 to 4 % above; ISO-NE's is 3 to 6 % below between 01:00 and 05:00 and within 1 % for the rest of the day; FPL's is 4 to 7 % below from 18:00 to 01:00. TVA, BPA and Duke show no offset (within 1 % at every hour). The EIA-930 instructions ask for "the day-ahead demand forecast generated in the normal course of business" and fix neither the hour of issue nor what the forecast quantity includes, so `DF` and `D` need not measure the same quantity.
3. **What it is worth.** Dividing `DF` by one plus its own median relative error at the same hour over the previous 60 days (known before the day) takes the demand-weighted MAPE of the eleven BAs from 3.04 % to 2.17 %, below the 2.59 % of the corrected ensemble on the same days. By BA the ensemble stays ahead of that corrected `DF` only at SPP (2.35 % against 2.53 %) and is close at PJM (2.35 % against 2.24 %).

`DF` as published stays the headline benchmark, because it is what a user of the public data receives. The corrected `DF` is reported next to it as the harder benchmark: it is closer to what the operators' forecasters achieve. It is not a forecast available at the 9 a.m. cut-off unless the BA publishes `DF` for the next day before that hour, which EIA-930 does not record.

## 5. Models

All models are fitted per BA, on a rolling origin: the last training day is two days before the target
day (the day before is known only until 08:00), models are refitted every 7 days, and the training
window is 730 days in phases B and C and 2190 days in phase D.

| Model | Specification | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Floors (`naive.py`) | the last 24 known hours placed at their hour of the day; the same weekday of the previous week; the mean of the same day type over the two weeks ending two days before | only fully known days |
| Profile (`profile.py`) | shape = median of the normalised analogue days (same day type and temperature regime; last 4 weeks plus 21 days either side of the same date a year before); level = median of the daily means of the 5 most recent analogues; regimes split at 59 and 72 F by the daily mean; with fewer than 3 analogues the regime is ignored | the profile of Note 2 made causal |
| Recency-effect regression (`recency.py`) | ordinary least squares on 215 regressors: trend, month, day type by hour, a cubic in temperature, the cubic by hour and by month, and cubics in the temperature lags of 1 to 3 h and in its moving averages over 6, 12 and 24 h | the benchmark model of the load-forecasting literature [3] with the recency effect [4]; temperature is divided by 100 to condition the matrix |
| Additive model (`additive.py`) | 24 ridge regressions, one per hour, after [5]: cubic B-spline bases with 6 interior knots at the quantiles of the training values for the temperature of the hour, the mean of the previous 24 h and the day's maximum and minimum, **plus a linear term for each**, the spline input being clipped to the training range; the load at the same hour of the last known day and of the same day a week before, the mean of the 24 hours before the cut-off, the day type, a trend; standardised columns; the penalty chosen in {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100} by MAPE on the last 60 days of the window, then a refit on all of it | the splines give the U-shaped response; beyond the training range the spline is constant and the linear term continues, which is what lets the model extrapolate into cold it has not seen |
| Gradient boosting (`boosting.py`) | histogram gradient boosting (scikit-learn 1.3 `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`): at most 400 iterations, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, early stopping on 10 % of the rows after 20 iterations without improvement, seed 0; hourly rows with the calendar, the temperatures and the known load; one temperature column per station when there are several | trees find interactions the regression writes by hand; they do not extrapolate beyond the training range |
| Features (`features.py`) | hour, day type, special-day kind, sine and cosine of the day of the year, trend; temperature, its lags of 1 to 3 h, moving averages over 6, 12 and 24 h, the day's mean, minimum and maximum; load at the same hour of the last known day (the day before up to 08:00, two days before after it), of the same day a week before, and the mean of the 24 hours before the cut-off | the same functions build the training rows (observed temperature) and the target rows (HRRR path), so there is no path for information to leak |
| Ensemble (`ensemble.py`) | the mean of the members; with recent weights, the inverse of each member's MAPE over the last 30 days whose outcome is known at the cut-off, measured on the forecasts the ensemble stored; equal weights until 5 such days exist; a member that declines a day is left out of that day | nothing of the target day enters the weights |
| Bias correction (`quantiles.EmpiricalIntervals`) | the median, per hour of the day, of the model's relative errors over the last 60 known days, applied to the forecast; at least 20 days | causal and cheap; found while building the intervals |
| Quantiles (`quantiles.py`) | boosting with the pinball loss at 5, 50 and 95 %, sorted to remove crossings; or the empirical quantiles of the same relative errors used by the bias correction | a minimal version of a 90 % prediction interval |

### Additions of phase E

The development set has eleven BAs from phase E on: the ten above and FPL (Florida Power & Light). The
FPL series contains a structural step inside the development period: the load of the former Gulf Power
area joins it in mid-July 2022 (about 11 %, measured against the other Florida BAs). The series is not
adjusted; a model has to absorb the step from the load lags, as it would in operation. FPL uses six
stations (Miami, West Palm Beach, Melbourne, Fort Myers, Sarasota, Pensacola).

## 6. Special days and daily shapes

The calendar (`grid_sentinel.calendar_us`) holds the federal holidays with their observance, the day
after Thanksgiving, 24 and 31 December and Super Bowl Sunday, each mapped to the day type it resembles
in Note 2. Three treatments were compared: substitution by that day type (the default), indicators of
the special-day kind on top of it, and a separate model fitted on special days and weekends (at least
30 days). Indicators were adopted; the separate model changes nothing.

Daily shapes (`clusters.py`, `docs/day_clusters.md`) are the 24 hourly values divided by the day's mean.
k-means (k-means++ seeding, 20 initialisations, seed 0) and the silhouette are implemented in numpy so
that the result is the same on any machine. Shapes are clustered **within each temperature regime**, k
between 2 and 5 by silhouette, because across regimes the clusters are the regimes themselves. The
distance to the nearest centroid flags atypical days; large documented load-shed days sit in the top
6 % of each BA, small ones do not. As a forecasting feature in place of the calendar day type, the
cluster label makes every model worse by about 0.9 point.

## 7. Cleaning the history

The control cleaning is the gross-fault guard of section 2. The alternative (`cleaning.py`) runs the
grid-data-sentinel composite with the BA's temperature as context, one year at a time, warmed up on
the year before, and repairs the flagged hours by equivalent days; the outcome is never cleaned. On the
ten BAs it changes the annual mean by 0.01 point.

## 8a. The recipe in one run

## 8d. Protocol v2

Three rules were added to the evaluation after the frozen run, all about the data rather than the models (`docs/phase_g_protocol.md`). A BA-day on which the published forecast is missing, non-positive or more than 50 % away from the outcome at any hour is a reporting fault of the benchmark and is left out for every model (34 BA-days of the development period, among them three NYISO days with a published forecast of zero). An hour more than 40 % away from the mean of its two neighbours is a fault in the outcome, and its day is left out (17 BA-days; the gross-fault guard of 0.2 to 5 times the recent median had let through FPL hours of 67 GW in a 15 GW system). And the documented hour-label offsets of the published series (`docs/hour_label_offsets.csv`: PJM's forecast is stamped one hour early from February 2019, CAISO's demand one hour late from 14 June 2022, both checked against the operators' own published series) are applied when the files are read, for the models and for `DF` alike. The recipe barely moves under protocol v2 (2.55 % to 2.54 % on the development period); the benchmark does (3.04 % to 2.69 %), because PJM's published error falls from 3.65 % to 2.45 % and CAISO's from 2.80 % to 2.38 %. Under protocol v2 the recipe is ahead of the published forecasts by 0.15 point over the eleven BAs (2.54 % against 2.69 %, better on 53 % of the days), ahead at PJM, SPP, MISO and NYISO, level at FPL, and behind at the other six. Protocol v2 is the evaluation used from here on; the numbers of sections 8a to 8c are kept as measured under the earlier rules, which is what a user of the published data would compute.

## 8g. Phase I: the extremes, and the whole distribution

Phase I asked what the recipe could still gain on the days that matter most, the winter events and the coldest mornings, once the annual MAPE had reached its plateau. |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The robust loss was not adopted: it lowers the conditional centre exactly where the outcome sits above it, and the tail measured in section 4b (shape 0.15 to 0.19) is too light for it to pay elsewhere. The wind block in the boosting, kept out in phase H because wind chill in the additive model cost more than the block gained, is neutral over the year and takes 0.13 point off the MAPE on the event days at nine of the eleven BAs, with the CRPS lower by 0.10 point and the Winkler score by 1.0 point there. ## 8i. Phase K: a second weather model

A model that errs more than the HRRR at the point and is warm in the cold dilutes the NBM, and an equal-weight mean has no way to know it; FPL, where the HRRR and the NBM both have trouble at coastal stations, is the one place a third opinion helps, and that stays as a note for a station-wise choice of models. Days before 1 January 2021 have no GFS and would be forecast as in `final6`. The recipe stays `final6`.

## 8l. Phase N: the weather models weighted per station

Phase M showed that an equal-weight mean has no way to know where each model is good: the GFS helped at FPL and hurt at SPP, and in phase L a heavier NBM helped at Duke and hurt at FPL. Phase N lets the station's own observations decide. For each station and each day, the temperature path is the mean of the models (HRRR, NBM, GFS) weighted by the inverse of each model's mean squared error at that station's observations over the hours of the last 30 days known at the cut-off on which every model and the observation exist (a Bates-Granger combination without covariances; `information_set.model_weights`, `cutoff.weighted_paths`); with fewer than 240 such hours the weights are equal. Each model's forecast "as seen" is the run available at each day's cut-off, so the errors are those a forecaster could have measured. Two refinements were tried on the six BAs of phase L (`docs/phase_n_weights3.md`, `docs/phase_n_weights2.md`, `docs/phase_n_weights3_cold.md`, `docs/phase_n_weights3_q.md`, and the `_strata` files):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| `final8b`: HRRR and NBM weighted, no GFS | 0.002 | 0.003 | worse by 0.008 | 0.015 | 0.025 | worse by 0.040 | 2 / 2 |

| `final8q`: as `final8`, the GFS left out on days below the station's cold decile | 0.016 | worse by 0.014 | 0.054 | worse by 0.002 | worse by 0.002 | 0.037 | 4 / 1 |

Differences against `final6` on the same days of 2020 to 2022, positive when `final8x` is better. Weighting the HRRR and the NBM against each other does nothing (`final8b`), as the fixed 0.7 weight of phase L had already said: the two err too alike for the weights to choose. All the gain of `final8` comes from the GFS entering where it helps (summer, the largest peaks, FPL by 0.15 and TVA by 0.06) and all its loss from the GFS entering on cold days, where the recent squared error does not see its rare warm failures; an absolute cold rule (`final8c`) barely fires in the Southeast. ## 8m. Phase O: a storm member, and the band on the coldest days

**A storm member.** The under-forecast at the peak on event days (-2.3 to -3.0 %) has not moved through phases I to N, and phase J had shown that weighting the cold days more inside the members does not move it either. Phase O tried a member of its own: a boosting model fitted on the days of the training window whose observed minimum is in the coldest decile (at least 40 such days), which answers only on a target day whose forecast minimum is at or below that threshold and is left out of the ensemble on every other day (`Boosting(cold_only=0.10)`; the same option exists for the additive model). On the six winter BAs (PJM, MISO, SPP, Duke, TVA, ERCOT) against the phase N recipe on the same days (`docs/phase_o_storm.md`, `docs/phase_o_storm_strata.md`): year 0.001 worse, event days 0.007 better (2 BAs better, 4 worse) with the peak under-forecast deeper (-2.98 to -3.09 %; ERCOT -3.99 to -4.92 %), the coldest mornings 0.007 better. On the days it answers the storm member alone errs 2.7 to 3.7 %, well above the ensemble on those same days, and it enters with about a quarter of the weight when it has no recent record: it dilutes rather than corrects. Not adopted. The reading that stays is that the peak under-forecast in the storms is not a lack of cold examples in the training set but the limit of what the point temperature forecast explains on those days.

**The band on the coldest days.** The 90 % interval covers 72 % of the hours on the event days and 80 % on the 30 coldest mornings of the development years (86 % in winter, 89 % over the year). Four changes to `AdaptiveIntervals` were tried on the same six BAs against the phase N recipe, which leave the point forecast untouched (`docs/phase_o_band_*.md` and the `_strata` files):

| Change on the days whose forecast minimum is in the coldest decile | Coverage, event days | Coverage, coldest mornings | Winkler, event days | Winkler, year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (phase N recipe) | 71.9 % | 79.7 % | 28.9 | 13.12 |
| `final10a`: the adaptive miscoverage level halved (a nominal 95 % band) | 73.9 % | 81.4 % | 27.3 | 13.14 |
| `final10b`: a separate adaptive level tracked on the cold days alone (step 0.10) | 73.6 % | 78.4 % | 28.8 | 13.14 |
| `final10a2`: the level quartered (a nominal 97.5 % band) | 75.0 % | 82.4 % | 26.4 | 13.16 |
| `final10a2t`: the level quartered and the outer quantiles read from an exponential tail fitted beyond the pool's 10th and 90th percentiles | 78.7 % | 85.0 % | 25.7 | 13.28 |

The separate adaptive level oscillates: fed by few, large updates it narrows the band after a well-covered cold day, which is what the coldest mornings cannot afford. The fixed scale works, with diminishing returns, because with 40 nearest days a 1.25 % quantile is the extreme of the sample and the band cannot pass the largest error seen; the exponential tail on those days lifts that ceiling, brings the event-day coverage from 72 to 79 % (up at all six BAs; Winkler down at all six) and the coldest mornings to the 85 % the study had set as its target, for 0.16 of Winkler over the year (1.2 %) and 0.56 in winter, since the tail widens all the cold-decile days and the storms are few of them. `final10a2t` was then run on 2023 to 2025 under protocol v2 on the eleven BAs (`docs/phase_o_frozen_band.md`, `docs/phase_o_frozen_band_strata.md`; the point forecast is that of `final8`, only the band changes): coverage on the event days from 82.0 to 90.5 % with Winkler from 18.6 to 18.0, on the 30 coldest mornings from 84.4 to 90.3 % with Winkler from 17.5 to 18.1, in winter from 87.7 to 88.6 %, over the three years from 89.2 to 89.3 % with Winkler from 12.97 to 13.13 (1.3 % more); CRPS unchanged. Coverage on the event days rises at all eleven BAs; Winkler falls where the band had been shortest (ERCOT 26.2 to 19.5 at 74 to 90 % coverage, SPP, FPL, MISO) and rises where it already covered its 90 % (TVA to 98 %, NYISO to 99 %, PJM to 94 %), because the rule widens every cold-decile day of every BA. Adopted as the band of the recipe: the two strata the study had listed as a limitation reach their target in both periods, for 1.3 % of Winkler over the year; the over-widening at the BAs whose band was already adequate is the limitation that replaces it (a scale per BA, or conditioned on the recent coverage of the cold days, is the next idea).

**The NBM spread as a width factor.** The NBM publishes, next to its temperature, the standard deviation of the blend's members (`tempstddev`, in degrees Fahrenheit), a measure of how uncertain the weather is on the day; it was extracted at the 68 stations for the 2020 to 2025 runs (`scripts/extract_nbm.py --element nbm_tempstd_f`). The loader gives each day its mean spread over the target hours and the BA's stations, and `AdaptiveIntervals(spread_beta=1)` multiplies the half-widths of the band (and of the extra quantiles) by the ratio of that spread to the median spread of the pool days, clipped to 0.7 to 2.0. On the six winter BAs against the phase N recipe on the same days (`docs/phase_o_band_s.md`, `docs/phase_o_band_s_strata.md`), the factor alone takes the coverage on the event days from 71.9 to 77.5 % and on the coldest mornings from 79.7 to 83.8 % and lowers Winkler in every stratum (events 28.9 to 27.1, coldest mornings 22.7 to 21.7, winter 15.6 to 15.2, year 13.12 to 13.02; CRPS 1.420 to 1.417): the band narrows on the days the blend agrees and widens on the days it does not, which is information, not just width. On top of `final10a2t` (`docs/phase_o_band_ast.md`, `docs/phase_o_band_ast_strata.md`) it adds 5.0 points of coverage on the event days (78.7 to 83.7 %, up at all six BAs) and 4.0 on the coldest mornings (85.0 to 89.0 %), and lowers the year Winkler from 13.28 to 13.22 and the winter one from 16.18 to 15.94, the first change to the band that costs nothing over the year. 
On 2023 to 2025 the three bands were run on the eleven BAs (`docs/phase_o_frozen_band_spread.md`, `docs/phase_o_frozen_band_spread_alone.md` and the `_strata` files; the point forecast is that of `final8` in all of them):

| Band, 2023 to 2025 | Coverage, event days | Winkler, event days | Coverage, coldest mornings | Winkler, coldest mornings | Winkler, winter | Winkler, year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| phase N (`final8`) | 82.0 % | 18.6 | 84.4 % | 17.5 | 13.96 | 12.97 |
| `final10a2t`: level quartered and tail on cold days | 90.5 % | 18.0 | 90.3 % | 18.1 | 14.62 | 13.13 |
| `final10ast`: the same plus the spread factor | 95.5 % | 19.9 | 93.4 % | 19.1 | 14.97 | 13.22 |
| `final10s`: the spread factor alone | 89.6 % | 17.7 | 88.6 % | 17.1 | 14.19 | 13.02 |

Stacked on the cold-day level and tail, the spread factor widens too far: coverage passes the target on the event days (95.5 %) and Winkler, which rewards coverage only up to the target, rises. Alone, it brings the event days to 89.6 % and the coldest mornings to 88.6 % with the lowest Winkler of the three in every stratum and the lowest CRPS on the event days (2.05 to 2.01 %), for 0.4 % of Winkler over the three years (1.3 % for `final10a2t`) and 1.6 % in winter (4.7 %). It is also the simpler mechanism: one factor read from a public forecast, no regime rule. **`final10s` is the band of the recipe**; `final10a2t` stays as the alternative for a system without the NBM spread, and as the sensitivity case. Both still widen where the band already covered its 90 % (TVA to 97 %, NYISO to 99.6 % on the event days), the limitation that stands.

**Location, not only width.** The calibration of the 23 issued quantiles (`docs/calibration_curves.md`, `scripts/calibration_curve.py`) shows the bands calibrated over the year (mean absolute calibration error 0.02) and the whole distribution shifted low on the event days: the median holds 36 % of the hours, the 90 % quantile 73 %. Widening cannot repair that. Re-reading every level 0.15 higher on the cold-decile days (`final10c`) moves the peak under-forecast on event days from -2.98 to -2.65 % and calibrates the median on the coldest mornings, but compresses the lower tail and loses coverage; translating every quantile by the same amount (`final10ct`) keeps the coverage and the peak gain but raises the CRPS on every cold stratum, because the cold decile holds about 36 days a year and most of them are not storms: a fixed move overshoots the ordinary cold day. A move scaled by the severity of the cold (`final10cs` on the stacked band, `final10scs` on the spread band; `docs/phase_o_band_cs_strata.md`, `docs/phase_o_band_scs_strata.md`) keeps the coverage, lifts the peak under-forecast on event days to -2.45 % and the event-day MAPE from 4.47 to 4.43 %, and calibrates the median on the coldest mornings, yet still raises the CRPS on every cold stratum (events 3.05 to 3.17 %): the move lifts the lower tail with the upper one, and on those days the load is rarely far below the median (`docs/calibration_curves_dev_s.md`). Not adopted; the finding that stays is that the storms are a shift of location that the point forecast does not see, and that the move has to be asymmetric.

## 8n. Phase P: the recipe on the 43 BAs of the station map

**Scope.** Every BA of the station map with data on 2023 to 2025: the eleven development BAs (one to five stations each) and 32 others with one station each (AEC, the 44th, has no data in the period). The recipe is `final10s` as fixed in phase O (point `final8`, band with the NBM spread factor); nothing was tuned for the new BAs, and the 18 stations that lacked HRRR dew point and wind were extracted before the run. Period 2023-01-01 to 2025-12-31 under protocol v2, the 9 a.m. cut-off and the same data rules. For the 32 new BAs this is the first and only run, so their numbers are out-of-sample in the strict sense; for the eleven it is the sixth look at these years. Reports: `docs/evaluation_43_bas.md` (the standard report), `docs/scoreboard_43_bas.md` (per BA against `DF`, `scripts/scoreboard_43.py`), `docs/evaluation_43_bas_strata.md` (strata on the 38 comparable BAs) and `docs/evaluation_43_bas_strata_all.md` (the 43).

**Comparability.** Five BAs (AVA, FPC, LGEE, PSEI, TEPC) publish a day-ahead forecast that the EIA lists as not comparable with their demand (Hourly Electric Grid Monitor, About, "Impact of pseudo-ties and dynamic scheduling on demand forecast": the forecast follows the BA's commercial arrangements, not its physical boundary; the list also names GVL, SEC and SPA, which are not in the station map). PSEI's `DF` runs 29 % below its demand, FPC's 23 %. The same page notes that EPE and PNM report in Arizona time, IPCO in Pacific time, and LGEE and MISO in Eastern standard time all year; the cut-off follows each BA's reporting clock, which for these five is not the civil local time. WACM ceased to be a BA on 1 April 2026, after the evaluation period. They stay in the evaluation of the forecaster, which is against the demand, and leave the scoreboard against `DF`. No BA was left out for its size: a cut by mean load would have removed 30 BAs before any of the five. WACM is not on the EIA's list, but its `DF` runs 17 % below its own daily peak on average, so its row against `DF` is read with the same reserve.

**Result.** On the 38 comparable BAs, on the days both scored (40 848 BA-days), the recipe's mean daily MAPE is 2.41 % against 3.16 % for `DF` weighted by demand, 2.86 % against 3.40 % at the median BA; it is better in 26 BAs and worse in 12 (24 and 11 at p < 0.05 by the Diebold-Mariano test on the daily MAPE). On the 27 comparable BAs it had never seen, 3.18 % against 4.14 % weighted, 19 better and 8 worse. On the eleven development BAs, 2.21 % against 2.90 %, 7 and 4. The coverage of the 90 % band is 89.2 % over the 43 BAs and between 88 % and 90 % in every one of them. The sixteen BAs where the recipe is ahead by more than one point are mostly western and southern systems with a weak published forecast (WACM 3.68 % against 15.41 %, NEVP 2.48 against 6.24, PNM 2.77 against 6.27, PSCO 3.95 against 6.02, CISO 2.62 against 5.38, SWPP 2.31 against 4.33); the twelve losses among the comparable BAs are systems whose own forecast is already very good (PGE 2.35 % against 1.33, SOCO 2.67 against 1.70, NWMT 2.81 against 1.96, ISNE 3.54 against 2.56, BANC 2.77 against 2.22, TPWR 2.88 against 2.41), AECI (4.16 against 2.91), and five within 0.2 of a point (EPE, BPAT, DUK, TVA, SC).

**By stratum (38 comparable BAs).** MAPE is lower than `DF` in every stratum: winter 2.59 % against 3.13, summer 2.39 against 3.28, shoulder 2.32 against 3.07, event days 3.30 against 3.63 (21 BAs better, 17 worse), the 30 coldest mornings of each BA 3.00 against 3.22, the 30 largest peaks 2.27 against 3.19. The peak is where the recipe does not win: the mean under-forecast at the daily peak is larger than `DF`'s on the event days (−2.43 % against −2.33) and on the largest peaks (−1.83 % against −1.41), although the worst single BA-day of the events is milder (−13.6 % against −22.5 in Gerri and Heather, −19.4 against −23.8 in January 2025). The BAs' forecasters, with their internal information, still read the peak of a storm or of a heat wave better than this recipe does, even where their whole-day error is larger.

**What this says about generalisation.** The recipe was built on eleven large systems and transfers to 27 others with one station each with the same qualitative result as on the eleven: ahead of `DF` on the year and in each season, by a margin that grows where the published forecast is weak and vanishes where it is strong, and behind at the peak of the extreme days. A single station is a thin description of a system like WACM or PACE; the station map and the per-BA losses are the first places to look in a second round.

## 8o. Phase Q: a second stage for the daily peak, not adopted

**Question.** On the 43 BAs the recipe under-forecasts the daily peak by less than the BAs' own forecasts on ordinary days and by more on the extreme days (section 8n). One candidate cause: the point is a weighted mean of members whose peaks fall at different hours, and the mean of misaligned curves has a lower maximum than its members.

**Candidates** (`src/ba_load_forecast/forecasters/peak.py`, `PeakStage`; group `peak_q`). Each multiplies the whole band of `final10s` by a Gaussian bump of 2.5 h centred on the hour of the forecast peak, so that the forecast peak is scaled by a ratio and the hours far from it do not move; each keeps its own copy of the adaptive band so that the adaptive level follows the band it issues. `final11m`: the ratio is the mean of the members' own daily maxima over the issued maximum, never below 1. `final11r`: a ridge regression, refitted every day on the known days, of log(actual / issued) at the hour of the actual peak, on features known at the cut-off (the members' flattening and disagreement at the peak, how far the forecast maximum and minimum sit beyond the station's known extremes, the NBM spread, day type, special day, and the median target of the last 14 known days); raw temperatures were left out after a smoke test showed the linear fit extrapolating them across seasons. `final11x`: `final11r` only on days of extreme forecast temperature.

**Rule fixed before the run.** Adopt a candidate only if the peak under-forecast improves both on the event days and on the 30 largest peaks of each BA in at least 7 of the 11 BAs, the yearly MAPE does not worsen by more than 0.02 point, the yearly CRPS does not worsen, and the yearly coverage stays between 88 and 91 %.

**Result** (11 development BAs, 2020-12-02 to 2022-12-31, 8277 BA-days, `docs/phase_q_peak.md`, `docs/phase_q_peak_strata.md`; peak under-forecast in percent of the actual peak):

| Stratum | `final10s` | `final11m` | `final11r` | `final11x` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| year | −1.23 | −1.20 | −1.24 | −1.25 |
| event days | −2.76 | −2.67 (8 BAs better, 0 worse) | −2.92 | −2.91 |
| 30 coldest mornings | −2.08 | −2.01 (8 better, 0 worse) | −2.28 | −2.27 |
| 30 largest peaks | −1.25 | −1.25 (3 better, 8 unchanged) | −1.52 (10 worse) | −1.53 (10 worse) |

MAPE, CRPS and coverage of `final11m` equal those of `final10s` in every stratum. None is adopted: the learned ratios worsen the largest peaks in 10 of the 11 BAs, and the members' maxima, harmless and slightly helpful in the winter strata, leave the largest peaks unchanged. **What it says about the cause:** the flattening of the mean is real but small, and it sits in the winter days with two daily peaks on which the members disagree; on the largest peaks, mostly summer afternoons, the issued maximum (already read at the 60th percentile near the peak) is above the members' maxima, so there is nothing to undo. The under-forecast on the extreme days comes from elsewhere: the weather input or the model's response at temperatures beyond its training days, which a run with observed temperature would separate.

## 8p. Phase R: the recipe with the observed temperature, a diagnostic

**Question.** Is the under-forecast on the extreme days a weather-forecast error or a load-model error? A run of the recipe in which every hour of each station's temperature path, from the cut-off to the end of the target day, is the temperature observed at that station (`Loader(oracle_temperature=True)`, group `oracle`, candidate `final10so`) answers it: what remains with a perfect temperature forecast belongs to the load model. Dew point, wind and the NBM spread stay forecast. Not a forecast; eleven development BAs, 2020-12-02 to 2022-12-31, compared with `final10s` of the phase Q run on the same 8277 BA-days (`docs/phase_r_oracle.md`, `docs/phase_r_oracle_strata.md`). Run on a second machine (`docs/reproduce.md`, step 70).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| year | 2.27 | 2.01 | −1.23 | −1.08 | 10 of 11 |
| winter | 2.47 | 2.17 | −1.48 | −1.23 | |
| summer | 2.35 | 1.98 | −0.93 | −0.81 | 9 |

**Reading.** A perfect temperature forecast takes about a ninth of the yearly error away (2.27 % to 2.01 %, better in 10 of 11 BAs): most of the error is the load model's, not the weather's. On the winter-storm days and the coldest mornings it closes about 30 % of the under-forecast at the peak: there the weather forecast matters. On the 30 largest peaks of each BA, mostly summer afternoons, the whole-day error falls (2.20 % to 1.77 %) but the under-forecast at the peak grows (−1.25 % to −1.40 %, worse in 6 of 11; the signed error at the peak goes from +0.13 to −0.65 % at PJM, +0.74 to −0.42 % at MISO, +0.03 to −1.27 % at NYISO): with the real temperature the models answer the hottest afternoons with too little load. The forecast temperatures had been hiding that by erring warm on those days. **The gap on the largest peaks is the load model's response at the top of its training range** (idea A30), and on the winter storms it is split between the weather (about a third) and the model (two thirds).

## 9. Limitations

One to five stations per BA. The 9 a.m. cut-off and the 2 h availability lag are conventions, not a
description of any operator's process. The HRRR and the NBM are weather models with their own errors and not the
forecasts any BA used; the BAs' forecasters have vendor weather and internal information this study
does not have. Special days, events and the ten BAs are choices of the study. Prediction intervals
are too narrow inside the storms (82 % coverage on 2023 to 2025) and slightly so on the coldest mornings (85 %). A large part of the margin over the BAs' published forecasts comes from offsets and, at PJM, a one-hour misalignment in the published `DF` (section 4a); against `DF` with its own hourly bias removed the models are behind at ten of the eleven BAs. The frozen evaluation covers three later years on the same eleven BAs;
the 32 BAs outside the development set were run once, with one station each (section 8n), and the recipe is behind the BAs' own forecasts at the peak of the extreme days there as on the eleven.
